## Q1 Title capitalization: MLA Handbook 9th ed., sec. 2.90 "Capitalizing Titles in English" (received 2026-10-10 11:08 PM ET)
- Title-style: capitalize first word, last word, and all principal words incl. after hyphens in compounds: nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, subordinating conjunctions (after, although, as, as if, as soon as, because, before, if, that, unless, until, when, where, while).
- Lowercase mid-title: prepositions (against, as, between, in, of, to, according to), coordinating conjunctions (and, but, for, nor, or, so, yet), "to" in infinitives, articles (a, an, the).
- Capitalize an article that begins a subtitle.
- Hyphenated prefix: don't capitalize the word after the prefix if the dictionary shows the combination as a closed word (example given: "Anti-slavery").
- Colon between title and subtitle (fig. 2.4).
- Untitled poem/short message: use first line or full text transcribed exactly (figs. 2.5, 2.6).

## Q2 Number ranges: MLA Handbook 9th ed., sec. 2.139 "Number Ranges", plus 5.89, 5.90, 5.91 (received 11:10 PM ET)
- Up to 99: second number in full (2–3, 10–12, 21–48, 89–99).
- Larger: only last two digits of second number unless more needed for clarity (96–101, 103–04, 395–401, 923–1,003, 1,003–05, 1,608–774).
- Roman numerals in full (ii–iii, xxi–xlviii). Alphanumeric in full (A110–A112).
- Years ≥1000 CE: drop the second year's first two digits if they match (2000–03, 1945–89); otherwise full (1898–1901). Years 1–999 CE follow the general rule (800–14). BCE years never abbreviated (748–742 BCE, 143 BCE–149 CE).
- Ongoing open range: space after the hyphen.
- Ranges use an en dash.
- 5.89: give the contained work's entire page range, or the single page.
- 5.90: "pp." before a range, "p." before a single page.
- 5.91: use the source's numeral style and case.

## Q3 Two authors
- Works Cited, sec. 5.7 (fig. 5.10): order as presented; reverse first name, comma, "and", second name normal order. Label after second name with comma ("..., editors."). Ex: "Dorris, Michael, and Louise Erdrich. The Crown of Columbus. HarperCollins Publishers, 1999." / "Gilbert, Sandra M., and Susan Gubar, editors. The Female Imagination and the Modernist Aesthetic. Gordon and Breach Science Publishers, 1986." (f8bdb1d0)
- In-text, sec. 6.5: both names; first prose mention full names; parenthetical surnames joined by "and": (Lemery and Auerbach 4–5). (22f7cf2b, 95942774, 9c25eb9d)

## Q4 Three or more authors
- Works Cited, sec. 5.8 (fig. 5.11): first name reversed, comma, "et al." Roman, italic only when referred to as a term. Ex: "Charon, Rita, et al. The Principles and Practice of Narrative Medicine. Oxford UP, 2017." (0060bb13)
- In-text, sec. 6.5: prose may list all or first + "and others"/"and colleagues"; parenthetical first surname + et al.: (Nickerson et al. 135).
- Sec. 6.6 corporate authors in parentheses: shortest noun phrase, drop initial article.

## Q6 Punctuation of entries, secs. 5.120–5.122
- Periods after Author, after Title of Source, at end of each container string, at end of entry. Commas mainly between elements within a container and between surname and first name.
- 5.121: multiple pieces of info in one element separated by commas. Exceptions: hyphen joins seasons/months in date (fall-winter 2018); number ranges en dash (nos. 3–4); journal spanning two volumes joined by "and"; copublishers slash (5.61). Supplemental elements end with a period, commas inside.
- Handbook examples END entries with URL/DOI followed by a period ("https://doi.org/10.1632/adfl.45.1.171." / "www.jstor.org/stable/40859548."). Examples drop https:// on URLs except DOIs shown as https://doi.org/….
- 5.122: supplied info in square brackets, "?" if uncertain ([1971?]); local newspaper city in brackets after name, not italic (Herald and Presbyter [Cincinnati]).

## Q5 Alphabetizing, secs. 5.123–5.127
- Order by first part of author name, or first eligible word of title for title-only entries.
- 5.124: LETTER-BY-LETTER. Inverted names, corporate and government authors with comma-separated units: alphabetize up to the comma; letters after comma only break ties. Ignore other punctuation and spaces in titles, descriptions, corporate names. Ignore initial A/An/The in titles; OMIT them for corporate authors. Ignore accents/diacritics (é=e); umlauts ignored for English audiences.
- Test fixture (handbook order): Achebe, Chinua; Beatles; Beowulf; Christine de Pizan; Descartes, René; De Sica, Vittorio; Duong Thu Huong; Executive summary; Film Crit Hulk; Gott; Götz; MacDonald, George; McCullers, Carson; MLA Handbook; Modern Language Association; Moonlight; Morris, Robert; Morris, William; Morrison, Toni; Saint-Exupéry, Antoine de; The Second Shepherds' Play; St. Denis, Ruth; United Nations; United States, Congress, House, Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence; United States, Department of Health and Human Services, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.
- 5.126: multiple works by one author: name in first entry only, then three em dashes (or three hyphens) + period + title; different role: dashes, comma, role (", translator."). Same-author works alphabetized by TITLE, role terms ignored.
- 5.127: coauthor entries starting with same name ordered by second author's surname (Scholes and Kellogg before Scholes and Rabkin). Same coauthors same order: three em dashes stand for all names.
